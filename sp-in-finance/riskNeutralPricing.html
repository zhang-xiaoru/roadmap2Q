22  Risk-Neutral Pricing

22.1 Change of measure

we wish to change how we measure probability from \(P\) to \(\hat{P}\) using Random Variable \(Z\).

Th let \((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\) be a prob space and \(Z\) be a a.s. non-negative. random variable with \(E(Z) = 1\). for \(A \in \mathcal{F}\), define \[ \hat{P}(A) = \int_A Z(\omega) \, dP(\omega) \] then \(\hat{P}\) is a probability measure

Coll let \(\hat{P}\) be the prob measure defined, and \(X\) be a nonnegative random variable, then \[ \hat{E}(X) = \int_\Omega X(\omega) \, d\hat{P}(\omega) = \int_\Omega X(\omega) Z(\omega) \, dP(\omega) = E(ZX). \] if \(Z\) is a.s. stricly positive, then \[ E(X) = \hat{E}\Big(\frac{X}{Z}\Big) \qquad \text{for all nonnegative R.V. } X. \]

Next, defines the meaning of equivalent between \(P\) and \(\hat{P}\)

Def. let \(P\) & \(\hat{P}\) be two prob-meas on \((\Omega, \mathcal{F})\), \(P\) & \(\hat{P}\) are said equivalent if they agree which set in \(\mathcal{F}\) have probability zero.

Remark:

  • Since the set of zero prob and set of prob \(1\) are complementary, equivalent \(P\) & \(\hat{P}\) agrees on event with prob \(1\) as well
  • \(P\) & \(\hat{P}\) defined in Th are equivalent
  • Equivalent means that two probability measure predict the same on the event that happens a.s. or a.s. not happens

In finance, the model has a sample space, where all possible future scenario lies. The probability measure for the event can be estimated through empirical data in real world, or in the price of derivatives securities. a risk-neutral measure \(\hat{P}\). The risk netural measure helps to solve the equation. though \(\hat{P}\) assign different prob to price paths, the equivalent property make sure that a hedging strategy works a.s. in \(\hat{P}\) will also work in \(P\)

Notice that from the fact \[ \int_\Omega X(\omega) \, d\hat{P}(\omega) = \int_\Omega X(\omega) Z(\omega) \, dP(\omega) \qquad \text{and} \qquad \int_\Omega Y(\omega) \, dP(\omega) = \int_\Omega \frac{Y(\omega)}{Z(\omega)} \, d\hat{P}(\omega) \] we can “regard” \(Z(\omega) = \dfrac{d\hat{P}(\omega)}{dP(\omega)}\) as in regular calculous, as a somehow “ratio of probability”. \(Z(\omega)\) is refered as Radon-Nikodym derivative.

22.2 Risk-Neutral Measure

Next, we perform similar change of measure. for the process. on \((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\) and filtration \(\mathcal{F}_t\), defined on \(0 \leq t \leq T\). let \(Z\) be the a.s. positive R.V. with \(E(Z) = 1\) and \(\hat{P}\) defined above. Then can define Radon-Nikodym derivative process as follow \[ Z_t := E(Z \mid \mathcal{F}_t) \]

Coll \(Z_t\) is a martingle

proof: let \(t > s \in [0,T]\), and \[ E(Z_t \mid \mathcal{F}_s) = E\big((E(Z \mid \mathcal{F}_t)) \mid \mathcal{F}_s\big) = E(Z \mid \mathcal{F}_s) = Z_s. \]

lemma: let \(t \in [0,T]\) and \(Y\) is \(\mathcal{F}_t\) measurable, then \(\hat{E} Y = E(Y Z_t)\)

proof: \(\hat{E} Y = E(YZ) = E[E(YZ \mid \mathcal{F}_t)] = E(Y E(Z \mid \mathcal{F}_t)) = E(Y Z_t)\)

lemma: let \(s, t\) satisfy \(0 \leq s \leq t \leq T\) and \(Y\) be a \(\mathcal{F}_t\) measurable R.V. then \[ \hat{E}(Y \mid \mathcal{F}_s) = \frac{1}{Z(s)} E(Y Z(t) \mid \mathcal{F}_s) \]

proof: \(\frac{1}{Z(s)} E(Y Z(t) \mid \mathcal{F}_s)\) is \(\mathcal{F}_s\) measurable, to show that it equal to conditional expectation, we need. \[ \int_A \hat{E}(Y \mid \mathcal{F}_s) \, d\hat{P} = \int_A \frac{1}{Z(s)} E(Y Z(t) \mid \mathcal{F}_s) \, d\hat{P} = \int_A Y \, d\hat{P} \qquad \forall A \in \mathcal{F}_s \] \[ \begin{aligned} \int_A \frac{1}{Z(s)} E(Y Z(t) \mid \mathcal{F}_s) \, d\hat{P} &= \hat{E}\Big(\mathbf{1}_A \frac{1}{Z(s)} E(Y Z(t) \mid \mathcal{F}_s)\Big) \\ &= E\big(\mathbf{1}_A E(Y Z(t) \mid \mathcal{F}_s)\big) = E\big(E(\mathbf{1}_A Y Z(t) \mid \mathcal{F}_s)\big) \\ &= E(\mathbf{1}_A Y Z(t)) = \hat{E}(\mathbf{1}_A Y) = \int_A Y \, d\hat{P}. \qquad \blacksquare \end{aligned} \]

22.3 Change of measure for 1D Brownian motion

Th (change of measure for 1D BM) Let \((W_t)_{0 \leq t \leq T}\) be a BM on a probability space \((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\) and \((\mathcal{F}_t)_{0 \leq t \leq T}\) be its filtration, and \((\Theta_t)_{0 \leq t \leq T}\) be another adapted process to this filtration, then define \[ Z_t = \exp\Big(-\int_0^t \Theta_u \, dW_u - \tfrac12 \int_0^t \Theta_u^2 \, du\Big) \] \[ \widetilde{W}_t = W_t + \int_0^t \Theta_u \, du \] assuming \[ E\Big(\int_0^T \Theta_u^2 Z_u^2 \, du\Big) < \infty \] Then, we have \(E(Z_T) = 1\) and \(\widetilde{W}_t\) is BM under \(\hat{P}\) defined above.

22.4 Stock under risk-neutral measure

consider a stock price \(S_t\) described by \[ dS_t = \alpha_t S_t \, dt + G_t S_t \, dW_t, \qquad 0 \leq t \leq T \] or in the integration form \[ S_t = S_0 \exp\Big(\int_0^t G_u \, dW_u + \int_0^t (\alpha_u - \tfrac12 G_u^2) \, du\Big) \] now consider an interest rate process \(R(t)\) and defines the discount process as \(D_t = e^{-\int_0^t R_u \, du}\). \(\implies dP_t = -P_t R_t \, dt\)

Discounted stock price. \[ D_t S_t = S_0 \exp\Big(\int_0^t G_u \, dW_u + \int_0^t (\alpha_u - \tfrac12 G_u^2 - R_u) \, du\Big) \] \[ \begin{aligned} d(P_t S_t) &= S_t \, dP_t + P_t \, dS_t + dS_t \, dP_t = -S_t P_t R_t \, dt + P_t(\alpha_t S_t \, dt + G_t S_t \, dW_t) \\ &= P_t S_t(\alpha_t - R_t) \, dt + G_t S_t D_t \, dW_t = G_t S_t P_t(\Theta_t \, dt + dW_t) \end{aligned} \] where \(\Theta_t = \dfrac{\alpha_t - R_t}{G_t}\) is the market price of risk.

Remark: for discounted stock price. the mean rate of return of the stock reduce while the volatility remains the same.

now we consider the discounted price in the prob measure \(\hat{P}\) defined in theorem with \(\Theta_t\) used the market price of risk. then \[ d(D_t S_t) = G_t D_t S_t \, d\widetilde{W}_t \iff D_t S_t = S_0 + \int_0^t G_u P_u S_u \, d\widetilde{W}_u. \] and thus the discounted Stock price is martingle under this measure. The new probability measure is called Risk-Neutral measure.

Stock price under risk-neutral measure. \[ dW_t = -\Theta_t \, dt + d\widetilde{W}_t \implies dS_t = R_t S_t \, dt + G_t S_t \, d\widetilde{W}_t. \]
