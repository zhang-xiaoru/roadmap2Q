20  Stochastic Integral

Published

March 17, 2026

To define the stochastic integral (specifically the Itô integral), we begin with an intuitive definition for simple functions, in a manner analogous to the definition of the Riemann integral.

20.1 Itô’s Stochastic Integral for Step Processes

Let \(0 = t_0 < t_1 < \dots < t_n = T\) be a partition of \([0, T]\).

Definition 20.1 (Step Process) A step process \((G_t^n)_{t \in [0, T]}\) defined on a partition \(0 = t_0 < t_1 < \dots < t_n = T\) is: \[ G_t^n = \sum_{k=0}^n \xi_{k} I_{\{t_{k} \le t < t_{k+1}\}} \] where \(\xi_k\) is \(\mathcal{F}_{t_k}\)-measurable, and indicator function \(I_{\{t_{k} \le t < t_{k+1}\}}\) is: \[ I_{\{t_{k-1} \le t < t_k\}} = \begin{cases} 1 & t_{k} \le t < t_{k+1} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \]

Definition 20.2 (Itô’s Stochastic Integral for Step Processes) For a step process \((G^n_t)_{t\in[0, T]}\) on partition \(0 = t_0 < t_1 < \dots < t_n = T\), we define the Itô’s stochastic integral \(I_t^n\) for \(t\in[t_k, t_{k+1}]\) as: \[ I_t^n := \int_0^t G_u^n \, dB_u = \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} \xi_i (B_{t_{i+1}} - B_{t_i}) + \xi_k (B_t - B_{t_k}) \] where \(B_t\) is a Brownian motion. We can also denote this as \(\mathrm{d}I_t^n = G_u^n \mathrm{d}B_u\).

Note

An intuitive understanding of this deifinition is to think \(t_0, \dots, t_n\) as trading days, \(B(t_i)\) as the price on day \(t_i\), and \(\xi_i\) as the number of shares bought at \(t_i\) and held until \(t_{i+1}\). The total gain of the asset (which would stands for an “integral”) would be \(\sum_{\text{trading days}} \text{shares} \times \text{price change}\).

Properties of step process integrals

Proposition 20.1 (Martingale Property)
\(I_t^n\) is a continuous martingale, with \(I_0^n = 0\).

Proof 20.1. Let \(0 \le s < t\). To show \(I_s^n = E(I_t^n | \mathcal{F}_s)\), or equivalently, \(E(I_t^n - I_s^n | \mathcal{F}_s) = 0\):

Suppose \(s \in [t_l, t_{l+1}]\) and \(t \in [t_k, t_{k+1}]\). Then: \[ \begin{align*} I_t^n - I_s^n &= \xi_k (B_t - B_{t_k}) + \dots + \xi_l (B_{t_{l+1}} - B_{t_l}) - \xi_l (B_s - B_{t_l}) \\ &= \xi_k (B_t - B_{t_k}) + \dots + \xi_l (B_{t_{l+1}} - B_{t_s}) \end{align*} \] Taking conditional expectations: \[ \begin{align*} E[\xi_k(B_t - B_{t_k}) | \mathcal{F}_s] &= E[E(\xi_k(B_t - B_{t_k}) | \mathcal{F}_{t_k}) | \mathcal{F}_s] \\ &= E[\xi_k E(B_t - B_{t_k}| \mathcal{F}_{t_k}) | \mathcal{F}_s] = 0 \end{align*} \] Here we have used the tower property, the fact that \(\xi_k\) is \(\mathcal{F}_{t_k}\)-measurable and the property of Brownian motion. Similarly argument is applied for all other terms except the last one. For the last term, we have: \[ E[\xi_l (B_{t_{l+1}} - B_s) | \mathcal{F}_s] = \xi_l E(B_{t_{l+1}} - B_s | \mathcal{F}_s) = 0 \] since \(\xi_l\) is \(\mathcal{F}_{t_l}\)-measurable thus also \(\mathcal{F}_{t_s}\)-measurable. Therefore, \(I_t^n\) is a continuous martingale.

Proposition 20.2 (Itô Isometry) \[ E[(I_t^n)^2] = E\left[\int_0^t (G_u^n)^2 du\right] \]

Proof 20.2. Let’s denote \(\Delta B_i = B_{t_{i+1}} - B_{t_i}, i < k\), and \(\Delta B_k = B_t - B_{t_k}\). Then: \[ \begin{align*} E[(I_t^n)^2] &= E\left[\left( \sum_{i=0}^{k} \xi_i \Delta B_i + \xi_k \Delta B_k \right)^2\right] \\ &= E\left[\sum_i \xi_i^2 (\Delta B_i)^2 + 2 \sum_{i \ne j} \xi_i \xi_j \Delta B_i \Delta B_j\right] \end{align*} \] For \(i \ne j\) (say \(i < j\)), \(\xi_i \xi_j \Delta B_i\) is independent of \(\Delta B_j\) (they are all \(F_{t_j}\) measurable), so \(E[\xi_i \xi_j \Delta B_i \Delta B_j] =E[\xi_i \xi_j \Delta B_i]E[\Delta B_j] = 0\). Thus, \[ \begin{align*} E[(I_t^n)^2] &= E\left[\sum_i \xi_i^2 (\Delta B_i)^2\right] = \sum_i E(\xi_i^2) E((\Delta B_i)^2) \\ &= \sum_i E(\xi_i^2)(t_{i+1} - t_i) = E\left[\int_0^t (G_u^n)^2 du \right] \end{align*} \]

Proposition 20.3 (Quadratic Variation) We defined the quadratic variation of \(I_t^n\) similarly as for Brownian motion: \[ [I_t^n, I_t^n] = \sum_i (\xi_i (B_{t_{i+1}} - B_{t_i}))^2, \] and \[ \langle I^n_t, I^n_t \rangle = E\left[\sum_i (\xi_i(B_{t_{i+1}} - B_{t_i}))^2\middle|\mathcal{F}_{t_i}\right] \] then we have: \[ \lim_{\|\Delta\| \to 0} [I^n, I^n] = \langle I^n, I^n \rangle = \int_0^t (G_u^n)^2 du \]

Note
  • \(\|\Delta\| = \max_{i} |t_{i+1} - t_i|\)
  • The convergence here is in \(L^2\) sense, which implies the convergence in probability.
  • The last interval is \(B_t - B_{t_k}\).
  • We can denote this in differential form as \((\mathrm{d}I)^2=(G_t^n)^2(\mathrm(d)B_t)^2=(G^n_t)^2\mathrm{d}t\).

Proof 20.3. \[ \begin{align*} \langle I^n_t, I^n_t \rangle &= E\left[\sum_i \xi_i^2 (B_{t_{i+1}} - B_{t_i})^2\middle|\mathcal{F}_{t_i}\right]\\ &= \sum_i \xi_i^2 E((B_{t_{i+1}} - B_{t_i})^2|\mathcal{F}_{t_i})\\ &= \sum_i \xi_i^2 (t_{i+1} - t_i)\\ &= \int_0^t (G_u^n)^2 du \end{align*} \]

The proof of convergence of quadratic variation is similar to the proof of convergence of Brownian motion quadratic variation.

Proposition 20.4 (Martingale Property 2) \[ (I_t^n)^2 - \int_0^t (G_u^n)^2 du \] is a martingale.

Proof 20.4. Need to show for any \(0 \le s < t\): \[ E[M_t | \mathcal{F}_s] = M_s \] where \(M_t = (I_t^n)^2 - \int_0^t (G_u^n)^2 du\).

That is, to show \(E[M_t - M_s | \mathcal{F}_s] = 0\). Compute: \[ \begin{align*} M_t - M_s &= \big((I_t^n)^2 - (I_s^n)^2\big) - \int_s^t (G_u^n)^2 du \\ (I_t^n)^2 - (I_s^n)^2 &= \big(I_t^n - I_s^n\big)^2 + 2 I_s^n (I_t^n - I_s^n) \end{align*} \] So, \[ E[M_t - M_s | \mathcal{F}_s] = E\left[ (I_t^n - I_s^n)^2 | \mathcal{F}_s \right] + 2 I_s^n E[I_t^n - I_s^n | \mathcal{F}_s] - E\left[\int_s^t (G_u^n)^2 du | \mathcal{F}_s\right] \] Since \(I_t^n\) is a martingale, \(E[I_t^n - I_s^n | \mathcal{F}_s] = 0\). Using the similar argument in Proof 20.2, we have: \[ \begin{align*} E\left[(I_t^n - I_s^n)^2 | \mathcal{F}_s\right] &= \sum_i E[\xi_i^2\Delta B_i^2|\mathcal{F}_s] = \sum_i E[\xi_i^2|\mathcal{F}_s](t_{i+1} - t_i) \\ &= E\left[\int_s^t (G_u^n)^2 du | \mathcal{F}_s\right] \end{align*} \] Thus, \(E[M_t - M_s | \mathcal{F}_s] = 0\). Therefore, \((I_t^n)^2 - \int_0^t (G_u^n)^2 du\) is a martingale.

20.2 Stochastic Integral for General Processes

To define \[ \int_0^T C_u dB_u \] for general \(C_t\), approximate \(C_t\) by step processes.

Let \(T\) be fixed.

Define: \[ L^2(0, T) = \left\{ (C_t)_{0 \le t \le T} : C_t \text{ is measurable w.r.t. } \mathcal{F}_t ,\quad E\left[\int_0^T C_t^2 dt \right] < \infty \right\} \]

There exists a sequence of bounded step processes \((C^n)_t \in L^2(0, T)\) such that \[ E\left[\int_0^T |C_u - C_u^n|^2 du \right] \to 0 \]

Definition: \[ Z_t = \int_0^t C_u dB_u := \lim_{n \to \infty} \int_0^t C_u^n dB_u \] where \((C^n)_t\) is a sequence of step processes as above. This is equivalent to \(dZ_t = C_t dB_t\).

Properties

  • \(Z_t\) is a continuous martingale.
  • Itô Isometry: \(E(Z_t^2) = E\left[\int_0^t C_u^2 du\right]\).
  • Quadratic Variation: \(\langle Z, Z \rangle_t = \int_0^t C_u^2 du\).
  • \(Z_t^2 - \int_0^t C_u^2 du\) is a martingale.
  • \(\int_0^t (dZ_u)^2 = \int_0^t C_u^2 du\). Justification: \[ (dZ_t)^2 = C_t^2 dt = d\langle Z, Z \rangle_t \]

Observation: \[ E[(Z_t - Z_s)^2 | \mathcal{F}_s] = E[Z_t^2 - Z_s^2 | \mathcal{F}_s] \] Letting \(s = t_{k-1}\) and \(t = t_k\): \[ E[Z_{t_k}^2 - Z_{t_{k-1}}^2 | \mathcal{F}_{t_{k-1}}] = E[\langle Z, Z \rangle_{t_k} - \langle Z, Z \rangle_{t_{k-1}} | \mathcal{F}_{t_{k-1}}] \]
