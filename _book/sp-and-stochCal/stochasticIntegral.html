20  Stochastic Integral

suppose we have a stock price \((X_t)_t\) and a trading strategy \((a_t)_t\), which essentially a Random variable that tells me how much share to hold. The How do I Calculate my total profit from \(0, to\ T\)

one simple model turn descret time into interval \(t_0 < t_1 < t_2 \cdots < t_n\), and we hold \(\xi_i\) # of share between \([t_{i-1}, t_i]\), decide at \(t_{i-1}\) (no future information) \(\Rightarrow \xi_i\) is \(\mathcal{F}_{t_{i-1}}\) measurable. and BM \((B_t)_t\) as price movement

Then the profit is \(I_t = \xi_1(B_{t_1} - B_{t_0}) + \cdots + \xi_n(B_t - B_{t_{n-1}})\)

when \(\sigma t = t_i - t_{i-1}\) is very small, this looks like an integral in regular calculous sense. However due to the QV of BM, the exact stochastic Calculous is more Complicated the intuitive argument above. but the general idea is similar.

we start with define the stochastic integral (Ito integral specificaly) for Simple step function \[ 0 = t_0 < t_1 < t_2 \cdots < t_n = T. \quad \text{a partition} \]

20.1 Step process

Def step process \(G_t\): \[ (G_t)_{t \in [0,T]} := \sum_{k=1}^{n} \xi_{k-1} \mathbf{1}_{\{t_{k-1} < t \leq t_k\}}, \qquad \xi_{k-1} \text{ is } \mathcal{F}(t_{k-1}) \text{ measurable.} \] \[ \mathbf{1}_{\{t_{k-1} < t \leq t_k\}} = \begin{cases} 1 & t_{k-1} < t < t_k \\ 0 & \text{other} \end{cases} \]

20.2 Itô’s stochastic integral (on step processes)

Def Itô’s stochastic integral (on step processes) \[ I_t^n = \int_0^t G_u^n \, dB_u = \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} \xi_i(B_{t_{i+1}} - B_{t_i}) + \xi_k(B_t - B_{t_k}), \quad t \in [t_k, t_{k+1}], \quad (B_t)_{t \geq 0} \text{ is BM} \iff dI_t^n = G_t^n \, dB_t \]

Remark: An intuitive under standy of this definition is to think \(t_0, t_1 \cdots t_n\) as the trading days, \(B(t_i)\) be the price at these days and \(\xi_i\) as number of shares acquired at date \(t_i\), hold tile time \(t_{i+1}\). Then the total gain of asset (which would stans for ‘integral’) would be \(\sum(\text{share} \times \text{price})\) all trades.

20.3 Properties of the Itô integral

Th \(I_t^n\) is a continous martingle with \(I_0^n = 0\)

proof: (1) let \(0 \leq s < t\), to show \(I_s^n = E(I_t^n \mid \mathcal{F}_s)\), we need to show \(E(I_t^n - I_s^n \mid \mathcal{F}_s) = 0\). suppose \(s \in [t_l, t_{l+1}]\), \(t \in [t_k, t_{k+1}]\), \(l \leq k\)

then \[ \begin{aligned} I_t^n - I_s^n &= \xi_k(B_t - B_{t_k}) + \xi_{k-1}(B_{t_k} - B_{t_{k-1}}) \cdots + \xi_l(B_{t_{l+1}} - B_{t_l}) - \xi_l(B_s - B_{t_l}) \\ &= \xi_k(B_t - B_{t_k}) + \cdots + \xi_l(B_{t_{l+1}} - B_s) \end{aligned} \] \[ E\big(\xi_k(B_t - B_{t_k}) \mid \mathcal{F}_s\big) = E\big(E(\xi_k(B_t - B_{t_k}) \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_k}) \mid \mathcal{F}_s\big) = E\big(\xi_k E(B_t - B_{t_k} \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_k}) \mid \mathcal{F}_s\big) = 0 \] \[ E\big(\xi_l(B_{t_{l+1}} - B_s) \mid \mathcal{F}_s\big) = \xi_l E(B_{t_{l+1}} - B_s \mid \mathcal{F}_s) = 0. \] \(\to I_t^n\) is continous martingle.

Th Ito isometry \[ E(I_t^{n2}) = E\Big[\int_0^t G_u^{n2} \, du\Big] \]

proof: Ito isomety \(E(I^2) = E\big((\sum_i \xi_i \Delta B_i)^2\big)\) \[ = E\Big(\sum_i \xi_i^2 \Delta B_i^2 + 2\sum_{i \neq j} \xi_i \Delta B_i \, \xi_j \Delta B_j\Big) \] when \(i \neq j\), assume \(i < j\), \(\Delta B_j\) is independent of \(\Delta B_i, \xi_i, \xi_j\) thus \(E(\xi_i \xi_j \Delta B_i \Delta B_j) = 0\) \[ \begin{aligned} E(I^2) &= E\Big(\sum_i \xi_i^2 \Delta B_i^2\Big) = \sum_i E(\xi_i^2 \Delta B_i^2) \\ &= \sum_i E(\xi_i^2) E(\Delta B_i^2) \\ &= E\big(\xi_i^2 (t_{i+1} - t_i)\big) = E\Big(\int_0^t G_s^{n2} \, ds\Big) \end{aligned} \]

Th The QV of \(I_t^n\) \[ [I_t^n, I_t^n] = \int_0^t G_u^{n2} \, du, \quad \text{and.} \qquad \langle I_t^n, I_t^n\rangle = \int_0^t G_u^{n2} \, du \]

proof: Calculate the QV on each piece wise interval. \[ \begin{aligned} [I_{t_i}^n, I_{t_i}^n] &= \lim_{|\sigma| \to 0} \sum_{j=1}^{m} (I_{h_j} - I_{h_{j-1}})^2, \quad \text{where } t_{i-1} = h_0 < h_1 \cdots < h_m = t_i. \\ &= \lim_{|\sigma| \to 0} \sum_{j=1}^{m} \xi_{t_{i-1}}(B_{h_j} - B_{h_{j-1}})^2 = \xi_{t_{i-1}} \lim_{|\sigma| \to 0} \sum_{j=1}^{m} (B_{h_j} - B_{h_{j-1}})^2 \\ &= \xi_{t_{i-1}}(t_i - t_{i-1}) \end{aligned} \] notice that the QV on \(0, t\) is then sum of all this \[ \text{thus} \quad [I_t^n, I_t^n] = \sum_j \xi_{t_{j-1}}(t_j - t_{j-1}) = \int_0^t G_u^{n} \, du. \] \[ \begin{aligned} \langle I_{t_i}^n, I_{t_i}^n\rangle &= \sum_{c=1}^{k} E\big[\xi_{i-1}^2(B_{t_i} - B_{t_{i-1}})^2 \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_{i-1}}\big] = \sum_{i=1}^{k} \xi_{i-1}^2 E\big[(B_{t_i} - B_{t_{i-1}})^2 \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_{i-1}}\big] \\ &= \sum_{i=1}^{k} \xi_{i-1}^2 (t_i - t_{i-1})^2 = \int_0^t G_t^2 \, dt. \end{aligned} \]

Remark: We can denote the integral form \(I_t^n = \int_0^t G_u^n \, dB_u\) in “differential form” \(dI_t^n = G_t^n \, dt\) and the QV \[ (dI_t^n)^2 = (G_t^n \, dB_u)^2 = G_t^{n2}(dB_u)^2 = G_t^{n2} \, dt. \] or. \(I_t^n\) accumulate \(G_t^{n2}\) QV per unit time.

Th Martingle Property \[ I_t^{n} - \int_0^t G_u^{n2} \, du \quad \text{is a martingle.} \]

Remark: the QV is computed path-by-path. chosing different \(a_t\) would leads to different integral. QV can be regarded. as a measure of risk, and it depends on the size of position \((a_t)\) we take.

20.4 From simple step process to General process

use \((G_t^n)_{t \in [0,T]}\), a step process to approximate any \(G\) to defines \(\int_0^T G_t \, dB_t\)

let \(T\) be fixed, \[ L^2(0,T) = \Big\{(G_t)_{0 \leq t \leq T}, \ G_t \text{ measurable w.r.t. } (\mathcal{F}_t), 0 \leq t \leq T, \text{ and } E\Big[\int_0^T |G_t|^2 dt\Big] < \infty\Big\} \] \[ \exists (G^n)_t \in L^2(0,T), \text{ bounded step process (sequence of) s.t. } E\Big[\int_0^T |G_u - G_u^n|^2 du\Big] \to 0. \]

Def let \(G_t \in L^2(0,T)\) and \((G^n)_t\) as bound sequence of step process above then \(L_t = \lim_{n \to \infty} \int_0^t G_u^n \, dB_u\) exist and defined as \(\int_0^t G_u \, du\).

Properties:

  • \(L_t\) is continous martingle, \(L_0 = 0\)
  • \(E(L_t^2) = E\big(\int_0^t G_u^2 \, du\big)\) — Ito isomety
  • \(\langle L_t, L_t\rangle = \int_0^t G_u^2 \, du = [L_t, L_t] := \lim_{\sigma \to 0} \sum_{k=0} (L_{t_k} - L_{t_{k-1}})^2\) — QV
  • \((L_t)^2 - \int_0^t G_u^2 \, du\) is a martingle
  • \(E(L_t) = 0\).

Justification on the QV. \[ E\Big[\big(\textstyle\sum_k (L_{t_k} - L_{t_{k-1}})^2 - \langle L, L\rangle_t\big)^2\Big] = E\Big[\big(\textstyle\sum_k \underbrace{((L_{t_k} - L_{t_{k-1}})^2 - (\langle L,L\rangle_{t_k} - \langle L,L\rangle_{t_{k-1}}))}_{A_k}\big)^2\Big] \] observation \(E[(L_t - L_s)^2 \mid \mathcal{F}_s] = E[L_t^2 - L_s^2 \mid \mathcal{F}_s]\), let \(s = t_{k-1}\), \(t = t_k\) \[ E\big[(L_{t_k} - L_{t_{k-1}})^2 \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_{k-1}}\big] = E\big[L_{t_k}^2 - L_{t_{k-1}}^2 \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_{k-1}}\big] = E\big[\langle L,L\rangle_{t_k} - \langle L,L\rangle_{t_{k-1}} \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_{k-1}}\big] \] as \(E[(L_{t_k} - L_{t_{k-1}})^2 \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_{k-1}}] = \langle L,L\rangle_{t_k} - \langle L,L\rangle_{t_{k-1}} = E[\langle L,L\rangle_{t_k} - \langle L,L\rangle_{t_{k-1}} \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_{k-1}}]\) \[ \Rightarrow E(A_k \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_{k-1}}) = 0 \] \[ E\big((\textstyle\sum_k A_k)^2\big) = E\big(\textstyle\sum_k A_k^2\big) + 2E\big(\textstyle\sum_{i \leq k} A_i A_k\big) \quad \text{since } E(A_i A_k \mid \mathcal{F}_{k-1}) = A_i E(A_k \mid \mathcal{F}_{k-1}) = 0 \text{ and } E(A_k^2) \text{ close to } 0. \]

Remark: again we denote \(dL_t = G_t \, dB_t\) and \((dL_t)^2 = G_t^2 \, dt\).

20.5 Itô processes and Itô’s chain rule

we then extend the integral process defined above to more generalise Ito processes.

Def - Itô processess let \((B_t)_{t \geq 0}\) a BM on \((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\) with filtration \(\mathcal{F}_t\) and some \((\mathcal{F}_t)_{t \geq 0}\), \((a_t)_{t \geq 0}\) sp adapted to \(\mathcal{F}_t\) and \(F_t \in L^1(0,T)\), \(G_t \in L^2(0,T)\) and \[ X_t = X_0 + \int_0^t F_u \, du + \int_0^t G_u \, dB_u \] is called the Ito process, which often denote in differential form \[ dX_t = F_t \, dt + G_t \, dB_t. \]

Remark: 1. the differential form is a notation for the integral expression, \(dX_t, dt, dB_t\) has no stand alone meaning

  1. Almost all sp, exlept the one with jumps (Levy-processes) are Ito process.

  2. Tthe \(F_t \, dt\) are refered as drift term (over all motion. due to external field) and Brownian term is called diffusive term.

Lemma: \([X, X]_t = \int_0^t G_u^2 \, dt\). \(\to\) QV come from BM, drift has no QV.

The natural Question next is to ask if there is a “chain” rule. in stochastic Integral that tell us \(d(U(X_t))\) is. The such rule in stochastic Calculous is called Ito’s chain rule. Similar to regular chain rule with extra term as result of none zero QV of BM.

Th - Itô’s Chain Rule. Suppose \(dX_t = F_t \, dt + G_t \, dB_t\) is an Ito processes defined above, let \(U: \mathbb{R} \times [0,T] \to \mathbb{R}\) and \(U(x,t)\) is continous and \(\frac{\partial U}{\partial t}, \frac{\partial U}{\partial x}, \frac{\partial^2 U}{\partial x^2}\) exists and continous, then \(Y_t = U(X_t, t)\) has diff eventtutw \[ \begin{aligned} dY_t &= U_t \, dt + U_x \, dX_t + \tfrac12 U_{xx}(dX_t)^2 \\ &= U_t \, dt + U_x(F_t \, dt + G_t \, dB_t) + \tfrac12 U_{xx} G_t^2 \, dt. \end{aligned} \]

Similar question can be asked, if there is a product rules in stochastic sense, and the answer is Yes, with also additional term due to QV of BM

Example: \(d(B_u^2) = 2 B_u \, dB_u + dt.\) \[ \begin{aligned} \int_0^t d(B_u^2) &= \lim_{|\sigma| \to 0} \sum_i (B_{t_i}^2 - B_{t_{i-1}}^2) = \lim_{|\sigma| \to 0}\Big(\textstyle\sum_i (B_{t_i} - B_{t_{i-1}})^2 + 2B_{t_{i-1}}\sum_i (B_{t_i} - B_{t_{i-1}})\Big) \\ &= \lim_{|\sigma| \to 0} \sum_i (B_{t_i} - B_{t_{i-1}})^2 + 2\lim_{|\sigma| \to 0} \sum_i (B_{t_i} - B_{t_{i-1}}) B_{t_{i-1}} \\ &= [B, B]_t + 2\int_0^t B_u \, dB_u = \int_0^t dt + \int_0^t 2 B_u \, dB_u \end{aligned} \] \[ \iff dB_u^2 = 2 B_u \, dB_u + dt \]

Example: \(d(t B_t)\) \[ \int_0^t d(u B_u) = \lim \sum_i (t_i B_{t_i} - t_{i-1} B_{t_{i-1}}) = \lim \sum_i t_i(B_{t_i} - B_{t_{i-1}}) + \lim \sum_i (t_i - t_{i-1}) B_{t_{i-1}} = \int_0^t u \, dB_u + \int_0^t B_u \, du \] \[ \iff d(t B_t) = t \, dB_t + B_t \, dt \]

Th - Itô’s product rule. let \(dX_t^1 = F_t^1 \, dt + G_t^1 \, dB_t\) be two Ito process on \((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\), \(dX_t^2 = F_t^2 \, dt + G_t^2 \, dB_t\) \[ \begin{aligned} \text{then} \quad d(X_1 X_2) &= X_2 \, dX_1 + X_1 \, dX_2 + dX_1 \, dX_2 \\ &= X_2 \, dX_1 + X_1 \, dX_2 + G_t^1 G_t^2 \, dt. \end{aligned} \]

lemma: \(dX_1 \, dX_2 = G_t^1 G_t^2 \, dt\)

proof let \(G_t^1, G_t^2, F_t^1, F_t^2\) has its Converging step sequence \(G^{1n}, G^{2n}, F^{1n}, F^{2n}\) and let \(X_n^1, X_n^2\) defined as. \[ X_n^1 = \sum_i F_{t_{i-1}}^1(t_i - t_{i-1}) + \sum_i G_{t_{i-1}}^1(B_i - B_{i-1}) \] \[ X_n^2 = \sum_i F_{t_{i-1}}^2(t_i - t_{i-1}) + \sum_i G_{t_{i-1}}^2(B_i - B_{i-1}) \] \[ \text{then} \quad \sum_i (X_{t_i}^1 - X_{t_{i-1}}^1)(X_{t_i}^2 - X_{t_{i-1}}^2) = \sum_i \big(F_{t_{i-1}}^1(t_i - t_{i-1}) + G_{t_{i-1}}^1(B_i - B_{i-1})\big)\big(F_{t_{i-1}}^2(t_i - t_{i-1}) + G_{t_{i-1}}^2(B_i - B_{i-1})\big) \]

taking \(|\sigma| \to 0\), the two drift-drift and drift-diffusion sums vanish (in \(L^2\) sense): \[ \lim_{|\sigma| \to 0} \sum_i F^1 F^2 (\Delta_i t)^2 = 0, \qquad \lim_{|\sigma| \to 0} \sum_i F^1 G^2 \, \Delta_i B \, \Delta_i t = 0 \] \[ \lim_{|\sigma| \to 0} \sum_i (X_i^1 - X_{i-1}^1)(X_i^2 - X_{i-1}^2) = \lim_{|\sigma| \to 0} \sum_i G^1 G^2 \, \Delta_i B^2 = \int_0^t G_u^1 G_u^2 \, du \]

proof: \[ \begin{aligned} \int_0^t d(X^1 X^2) &= \lim_{|\sigma| \to 0} \sum_i (X_{t_i}^1 X_{t_i}^2 - X_{t_{i-1}}^1 X_{t_{i-1}}^2) \\ &= \lim_{|\sigma| \to 0} \sum_i \big[X_{t_{i-1}}^1(X_{t_i}^2 - X_{t_{i-1}}^2) + X_{t_{i-1}}^2(X_{t_i}^1 - X_{t_{i-1}}^1) + (X_{t_i}^1 - X_{t_{i-1}}^1)(X_{t_i}^2 - X_{t_{i-1}}^2)\big] \\ &= \int_0^t X_u^1 \, dX_u^2 + \int_0^t X_u^2 \, dX_u^1 + \int_0^t G_u^1 G_u^2 \, du \end{aligned} \]

Def \(dX_t = F_t \, dt + G_t \, dB_t\), and \((a_t)_t\) be a adapted process \[ \int_0^t a_u \, dX_u := \int_0^t a_u F_u \, du + \int_0^t a_u G_u \, dB_u. \] or. \(a_t \, dX_t = a_t F_t \, dt + a_t G_t \, dB_t.\)

proof - Itô’s Chain Rule. using Itô’s product, an proof chainrule

① using product rule can show for \(U(x) = x^m\), we ①,\(\cdots\) \[ dX_t^m = m X^{m-1} \, dX_t + \tfrac12 m(m-1) X^{m-2} G_t^2 \, dt. \] (using induction), which satisfy the chain rule. The linearity of integration give that all polynomial satisfied.

② then we show for \(f(x), g(t)\) that are both polynomial, \[ \begin{aligned} d(U(X_t)) = d[f(x) g] &= f(x) \, dg + g(t) \, df(x) \\ &= f(x) g' \, dt + g(t)\big(f'(x) \, dx + \tfrac12 f''(x) G_t^2 \, dt\big) \\ &= \frac{\partial U}{\partial t} \, dt + \frac{\partial U}{\partial x}(x) \, dx + \tfrac12 \frac{\partial^2 U}{\partial x^2} G_t^2 \, dt. \end{aligned} \] due to linearity, \(U(x,t) = \sum_i f_i(x) g_i(t)\) also Satisfies Ito formula. That is, all polynomial function \(U\) of \(x, t\) satis fy.

③ For then \(U\) in Ito’s chain rule statement, \(\exists (U^n)_{n=0,1,\cdots}\), ’a sequence of polynomial function s.t. \(U^n \to U, U_t^n \to U_t, U_x^n \to U_x, U_{xx}^n \to U_{xx}\) uniformly on compact subset of \(\mathbb{R} \times [0,T]\). \[ \text{sun} \quad \int_0^t dU^n = \int_0^t U_t^n \, du + \int_0^t U_x^n \, dX + \tfrac12 \int_0^t U_{xx}^n G_u^2 \, du \quad \text{a.s.} \] passing the limit yet chain rule in general.

Summary: The way remember this is to reconize that \(dB_t \, dB_t = dt.\) instead of \(0\), as in regular calculus \(\ dB_t \approx \sqrt{dt}\)

  • then for product \[ \begin{aligned} d X_1 X_2 &= X_1 \, dX_2 + X_2 \, dX_1 + dX_1 \, dX_2 \\ &= X_1 \, dX_2 + X_2 \, dX_1 + (F^1 dt + G^1 dB)(F^2 dt + G^2 dB) \\ &= \cdots + G^1 G^2 \, dt \end{aligned} \] the result we higher order term, ignored as usual.

  • Itô’s chain rule then can be view as lowest order Tyler expansion. \[ f(X_t) = f(0,0) + \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} \, dX_t + \frac{\partial f}{\partial t} \, dt + \tfrac12 \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2}(dX_t)^2. \] as \((dX_t)^2 = G_t^2 \, dt\), and should be keep.

20.6 General Itô’s chain rule

Itô’s chain rule can be similarly extend to multip variable.

let \(dX_t^i = F_t^i \, dt + G_t^i \, dB_t\) on \((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\) with \(F^i \in L^1(0,T)\), \(G^i \in L^2(0,T)\) adapted, \(i = 1, \cdots n\), and \(u: \mathbb{R}^n \times [0,T] \to \mathbb{R}\) is continous, with continous \(u_t, u_{x_i}, u_{x_i x_j}\) \(\forall t_i\), then \[ d u(x^1, x^2, \cdots, t) = u_t \, dt + \sum_i \frac{\partial u}{\partial x_i} \, dX_t^i + \tfrac12 \sum_{ij} \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x_i \partial x_j} G_t^i G_t^j \, dt \]

20.7 Examples

1. Geometric Brownian Motion. \[ dX_t = G_t \, dB_t + (\alpha_t - \tfrac12 \sigma^2) \, dt, \qquad B_t \text{ is BM w.r.t. } \mathcal{F}_t \text{ filtration and } G_t, \alpha_t \text{ be adapted process.} \]

and consider an asset price given by \[ S_t = S_0 \exp(X_t) \] \[ \begin{aligned} \text{then} \quad dS_t &= d(S_0 \exp(X_t)) = S_0\big(\exp(X_t) \, dX_t + \tfrac12 \exp(X_t) \, dX_t \, dX_t\big) \\ \text{and} \quad dX_t \, dX_t &= G_t^2 (dB_t)^2 = G_t^2 \, dt. \\ \Rightarrow \quad dS_t &= S_0 \exp(X_t)\big(G_t \, dW_t + (\alpha_t - \tfrac12 \sigma^2) \, dt\big) + \tfrac12 S_0 \exp(X_t) G_t^2 \, dt \\ &= \alpha_t S_t \, dt + G_t S_t \, dB_t. \end{aligned} \]

the asset price would have instantaneous mean rate of return. \(\alpha_t\) and volatility \(G(t)\).

This example includes all possible asset price model that is always positive, has no jumps and driven by a single BM.
