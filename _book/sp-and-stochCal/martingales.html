17  Martingales

fair game: Consider \(Y_1, Y_2 \cdots\) are independent “fair” gambling game, \(E(Y_i) = 0\).

\(S_n := Y_1 + Y_2 \cdots \cdots + Y_n\) as accumulated. total wining

\(Q\): what is the best guess of \(S_{n+k}\) given \(S_1, S_2 \cdots S_n\)?

\[ \begin{aligned} E(S_{n+k} \mid S_1, S_2, \cdots S_n) &= E(Y_1 + Y_2 \cdots + Y_{n+k} \mid S_1, S_2, \cdots S_n) \\ &= \underline{E(Y_1 + Y_2 + \cdots + Y_n \mid S_1, \cdots S_n)} + E(Y_{n+1} + \cdots Y_{n+k} \mid S_1, \cdots S_n) \\ &= Y_1 + Y_2 + \cdots + Y_n + E(Y_{n+1} + \cdots + Y_{n+k} \mid S_1, \cdots S_n) \\ &= Y_1 + Y_2 + \cdots + Y_n + E(Y_{n+1} + \cdots + Y_{n+k}) \\ &= S_n \end{aligned} \]

Thus, for a fair game, the best estimate of future value \(S_{n+k}\) given the history up to \(n\), is just \(S_n\).

17.1 Discrete time martingle

Def. consider probability space \((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\). \((X_m)_{m=0,1,\cdots}\) be a stochastic process, i.e. \(X_m\) is R.V. for each \(m \geq 0\), and integrable \((E(|X|) < \infty)\)

Then \((X_m)_{m=0,1,\cdots}\) is called a Martingle if \[ X_m = E(X_{m+1} \mid X_0, X_1, \cdots X_m), \quad \text{for } \forall m \geq 0 \]

a submartingle if \[ X_m \leq E(X_{m+1} \mid X_0, \cdots X_m), \quad \text{for } \forall m \geq 0 \]

a supmartingle if \[ X_m \geq E(X_{m+1} \mid X_0, \cdots X_m) \quad \text{for } \forall m \geq 0 \]

Remark: A martingle means that if we have the observation of the history of the process \(X_0, \cdots X_m\), then the observed. value of present is the expected value of future.

17.2 Filtration

In many practical example, our observation may not be just \(X\), for example if we have a sequence \((X_m, Y_m)_{m=0,1,\cdots}\) where \(X_m, Y_m\) might be correlated, in this case, it is reasonable to define martingle as \[ X_m = E(X_{m+1} \mid (X_0, Y_0), \cdots (X_m, Y_m)) \quad \text{or} \quad = E(X_{m+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_m) \] where each \((Y_m, X_m)\) is measurable w.r.t. to \(\mathcal{F}_m\), and of course \(X_m\)

we thus can redefine martingle under a more generalized language, we we. introduce the concept of filtration

Def: a set of sub-\(\sigma\)-field \(\{\mathcal{F}_n\}\) on \((\Omega, \mathcal{F})\) is called a filtration s.t. \(\mathcal{F}_0 \subseteq \mathcal{F}_1 \subseteq \mathcal{F}_2 \subseteq \cdots \mathcal{F}_n \subseteq \mathcal{F}\), \(\forall \mathcal{F}_n\) is \(\sigma\)-field

Remarks: A filtration represents any procedure of collecting more and more information as time goes on.

Def: let \((X_m)_m\) on \((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\) and \((\mathcal{F}_m)_m\) be a filtration. we called. \((X_m)_m\) is adapted to \((\mathcal{F}_m)\) if \(\sigma(X_n) \subseteq \mathcal{F}_n\)

17.3 Martingle w.r.t. filtration.

consider \((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\), \((X_m)_{m=0,1,\cdots}\) and filtration \((\mathcal{F}_m)_{m=0,1,\cdots}\) then \((X_m)_m\) is called a martingle w.r.t. to filtration \((\mathcal{F}_m)_m\) if

  • \(\forall m \geq 0\), \(X_m\) is \(\mathcal{F}_m\) measurable. (adapted)
  • \(X_m = E(X_{m+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_m)\)

Remarks: This definition keep the essense of martingle that when the past information is known (conditional), the present value is the expected future value.

Lemma: let \((X_m)_m\) a martingle w.r.t. \((\mathcal{F}_m)_m\) on \((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\) then \(X_m = E(X_n \mid \mathcal{F}_m)\), \(\forall n > m\).

proof: \[ X_m = E[X_{m+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_m] = E[E(X_{m+2} \mid \mathcal{F}_{m+1}) \mid \mathcal{F}_m]. \quad \text{Since } \mathcal{F}_m \subseteq \mathcal{F}_{m+1} \] \[ = E[X_{m+2} \mid \mathcal{F}_m] \] continous this process can show the lemma.

17.4 Natural filtration

prop: suppose we have discrete time process \(X_0, X_1, \cdots X_m \cdots\), we can define \(\mathcal{F}_m^X := \sigma(X_0, X_1, \cdots X_m)\), \(m = 0, 1 \cdots\) natural filtration of \((X_m)_m\), \((\mathcal{F}_m^X)\) is the smallest filtration s.t. \((X_m)_m\) is adaptive.

Remark: \(\mathcal{F}_m^X\) is the \(\sigma\)-algebra generated by previous history \((X_0, X_1, \cdots X_m)\), i.e. the smallest ‘information’ of the entire history.

proof: need to show that if \(X_m\) is \(\mathcal{F}_m\) measurable, then \(\mathcal{F}_m^X \subseteq \mathcal{F}_m\), \(\forall m\).

suppose we have \(\sigma(X_0) \subseteq \mathcal{F}_0\), \(\sigma(X_1) \subseteq \mathcal{F}_1\), \(\cdots \sigma(X_m) \subseteq \mathcal{F}_m\). and \(\mathcal{F}_0 \subseteq \mathcal{F}_1 \subseteq \mathcal{F}_2 \cdots \subseteq \mathcal{F}_m\).

then we know \(\sigma(X_0) \subseteq \mathcal{F}_0 \subseteq \mathcal{F}_m\), \(\sigma(X_1) \subseteq \mathcal{F}_1 \subseteq \mathcal{F}_m\), \(\cdots \sigma(X_m) \subseteq \mathcal{F}_m\).

i.e. \(X_0, \cdots X_m\) are all \(\mathcal{F}_m\) measurable.

thus \(\sigma(X_0 \cdots X_m) = \mathcal{F}_m^X \subseteq \mathcal{F}_m\).

17.5 Martingle Transformation.

The martingle transformation of a discrete martingle can be viewed as a “discrete” form of later defined stochastic Integral

Consider a discrete model of stock price \((X_m)_{m=0,1,\cdots 2}\) adaptive to \((\mathcal{F}_m)_{m=0,1,\cdots}\). let \(U_m := \#\) of share from \(m-1\) to \(m\). This should be \((\mathcal{F}_{m-1})\) measurable, as we only know the information till \(m-1\) to make decision how much to hold till next day. Then my profit would be \[ U_1(X_1 - X_0) + U_2(X_2 - X_0) + \cdots + U_n(X_n - X_{n-1}) \]

The formal definition of martingale transformation is as follows

Def let \((X_m)_{m=0,\cdots}\) on \((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\), adapted to \((\mathcal{F}_m)_m\), let \((U_m)_{m=1,2,\cdots}\) measurable respet to \(\mathcal{F}_{m-1}\)

let \[ Z_m = U_1(X_1 - X_0) + U_2(X_2 - X_1) + \cdots + U_m(X_m - X_{m-1}) \] \[ Z_0 = 0 \] \((Z_m)_{m=0,1,2,\cdots}\) is called the martingle transformation of \(X_m\).

As the name suggest, the martingle transformation transform a martingle to another martingle

Th: if \((X_m)_m\) is a martingle and \((Z_m)_m\) is \(\mathcal{F}_{m-1}\) measurable \(\forall m = \{0, 1, 2, \cdots\}\), then \((Z_m)_m\) is a martingle.

Proof: for a martingle \(M_m = E(M_{m+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_m) \iff E(M_{m+1} - M_m \mid \mathcal{F}_m) = 0\). \[ \begin{aligned} E(Z_{m+1} - Z_m \mid \mathcal{F}_m) &= E(U_{m+1}(X_{m+1} - X_m) \mid \mathcal{F}_m) \\ &= U_{m+1} E(X_{m+1} - X_m \mid \mathcal{F}_m) = 0. \qquad \blacksquare \end{aligned} \]
