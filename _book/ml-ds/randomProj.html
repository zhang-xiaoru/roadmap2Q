15  Randomized Projection

Idea: Compress high dimension data to lower dimension to reduce computation / storage.

Approach: map the original data \(X \in \mathbb{R}^d\) into \(\mathbb{R}^r\), with \(r < d\), while maintaining their pairwise distance unchanged (or at least approximately).

15.1 Projection map \(\phi : \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}^r\)

Let the original data lie in \(\mathbb{R}^d\), and \(S_r\) an \(r\)-dimensional subspace of \(\mathbb{R}^d\) with orthonormal basis \((v_1, v_2, \dots, v_r)\) spanning \(S_r\).

Definition 15.1 (Linear projection map) A linear projection map \(\phi : \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}^r\) is defined as

\[ Y = \phi(x) = \big(\langle x, v_1\rangle,\ \langle x, v_2\rangle,\ \dots,\ \langle x, v_r\rangle\big)^{\mathsf{T}} = \begin{pmatrix} -\,v_1^{\mathsf{T}}\,- \\ -\,v_2^{\mathsf{T}}\,- \\ \vdots \\ -\,v_r^{\mathsf{T}}\,- \end{pmatrix} x = \Phi\, x, \quad \Phi \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d},\ x \in \mathbb{R}^d,\ y \in \mathbb{R}^r . \]

A random projection map \(\phi\) is when \(S_r\) is a random \(r\)-dimensional subspace.

15.2 Approx. distance preservation

\(y_i = \Phi x_i\), we wish for small \(\varepsilon > 0\)

\[ (1-\varepsilon)\,\|x_i - x_j\|_2^2 \ \le\ \|y_i - y_j\|_2^2 \ \le\ (1+\varepsilon)\,\|x_i - x_j\|_2^2, \qquad \forall\, i, j \in \{1, \dots, n\}. \]

15.3 Gaussian Projectors

Consider random projection \(\phi = \begin{pmatrix} -\,u_1^{\mathsf{T}}\,- \\ -\,u_2^{\mathsf{T}}\,- \\ \vdots \\ -\,u_r^{\mathsf{T}}\,- \end{pmatrix}\) where \(u_i \in \mathbb{R}^d\) and \(u_i \overset{\text{iid}}{\sim} N(0, I_d)\) spherical Gaussian vectors.

Theorem 15.1 (Gaussian projection) For \(\forall v \in \mathbb{R}^d\), \(\exists\, c > 0\) s.t. \(\forall \varepsilon \in (0,1)\)

\[ P\Big(\big|\,\|\phi v\|_2 - \sqrt{r}\,\|v\|_2\,\big| \ge \varepsilon \sqrt{r}\,\|v\|_2 \Big) \le 3 e^{-c r \varepsilon^2}. \]

Remark. The projected vector \(\phi v\) will be close to \(\sqrt{r}\,\|v\|\) length, with high probability.

Proof. \[ \phi_{ij} \sim N(0,1) \;\Rightarrow\; \phi_{ij} v_j \sim N(0, v_j^2) \;\Rightarrow\; \sum_j \phi_{ij} v_j \sim N(0, \|v\|^2) \]

thus

\[ \frac{1}{\|v\|}\sum_j \phi_{ij} v_j \sim N(0,1) \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{1}{\|v\|}(\phi v)_i \sim N(0,1) \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{1}{\|v\|}\phi v \sim N(0, I_r) \]

using the Gaussian Annulus Theorem.

\[ \begin{align} P\Big(\big|\,\|\frac{1}{\|v\|_2}\phi v\|_2 - \sqrt{r}\,\,\big| \ge \varepsilon\sqrt{r}\, \Big) &\le 3 e^{-c r \varepsilon^2} \\ \Rightarrow P\Big(\big|\,\|\phi v\|_2 - \sqrt{r}\,\|v\|_2\,\big| \ge \varepsilon\sqrt{r}\,\|v\|_2 \Big) &\le 3 e^{-c r \varepsilon^2}. \end{align} \]

15.4 Johnson–Lindenstrauss lemma

Lemma 15.1 (Johnson–Lindenstrauss) Let \(x_1, \dots, x_n \in \mathbb{R}^d\), and Gaussian random projection \(\phi = \begin{pmatrix} -\,u_1^{\mathsf{T}}\,- \\ -\,u_2^{\mathsf{T}}\,- \\ -\,u_r^{\mathsf{T}}\,- \end{pmatrix}\). then \(\exists\, c > 0\) s.t. \(\forall \varepsilon \in (0,1)\) and \(\delta > 0\), if

\[ r \ge \frac{c}{\varepsilon^2}\,\log\!\left(\frac{3 n^2}{\delta}\right), \]

then

\[ P\Big(\big|\,\|\phi (x_j - x_k)\| - \sqrt{r}\,\|x_j - x_k\|\,\big| \le \varepsilon \sqrt{r}\,\|x_j - x_k\| \Big) \ge 1 - \delta, \qquad \forall\, j, k . \]

Remark. The JL lemma states that the distance between projected points

\[ (1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{r}\,\|x_i - x_j\| \ \le\ \|\phi(x_i - x_j)\| \ \le\ (1+\varepsilon)\sqrt{r}\,\|x_i - x_j\| \]

with high probability.

Proof. Let \(v_{ij} = x_i - x_j\), from the Gaussian projection theorem (Theorem 15.1), we know \(\exists\, c_{ij},\ \varepsilon_{ij} \in (0,1)\) s.t.

\[ P\Big(\big|\,\|\phi v_{ij}\| - \sqrt{r}\,\|v_{ij}\|\,\big| \ge \varepsilon_{ij}\sqrt{r}\,\|v_{ij}\| \Big) \le 5 e^{-c_{ij} r \varepsilon_{ij}^2}. \]

to show JL lemma, we need a union bound of event

\[ E_{ij}:\quad \big|\,\|\phi v_{ij}\| - \sqrt{r}\,\|v_{ij}\|\,\big| \ge \varepsilon_{ij}\sqrt{r}\,\|v_{ij}\| \]

so that above is true for \(\forall i, j\).

\[ P\Big(\bigcup_{ij} E_{ij}\Big) \le \sum_{ij} P(E_{ij}) \le \sum_{ij} 3 e^{-c_{ij} r \varepsilon_{ij}^2} \le \binom{n}{2} e^{-c r \varepsilon^2} 3 \]

where \(c = \min\{c_{ij}\}\), \(\varepsilon = \min\{\varepsilon_{ij}\}\), \(\sum_{ij}\) has \(\binom{n}{2}\) terms. Therefore

\[ P\Big(\bigcup_{ij} E_{ij}\Big) \le 3\binom{n}{2} e^{-c r \varepsilon^2} \le 3\tfrac{1}{2} n^2 e^{-c r \varepsilon^2} \le \delta \]

\[ \Rightarrow\ \delta \ge 3 n^2 e^{-c r \varepsilon^2} \ \Rightarrow\ r \ge \frac{1}{c \varepsilon^2}\,\log\!\left(\frac{3n^2}{\delta}\right). \]

15.5 Computing Random Projection

Direct computing requires \(O(nrd)\) time, \(n\) is sample size.

Idea: if \(X = (x_1, \dots, x_d)\) has iid entry, we can randomly select \(r\) entry and approximate \(\|x\|^2_2 \approx \tfrac{d}{r}\sum_{k}^rv_{ik}\), where \(i_k\) is randomly selected in \(\{1,2,\cdots,d\}\). Thus \(\phi = \sqrt{\tfrac{d}{r}}\, P\), where \(P\) is a random selection matrix should approximatly satisfy \(\|\phi x\|_2 \approx \|x\|_2\).

Issue: \(X\) is usually not iid entry, but tends to be sparse.

Fix: consider if \(x\) is sparse (\(\delta\)-function peaks), its Fourier transform would be smooth (sin function). We can find a similar transform matrix s.t. the transformed \(X\) is flat.

15.6 Hadamard transform

Definition 15.2 (Hadamard transform) Hadamard transform matrix \(H_m \in 2^m \times 2^m\) is defined recursively as

\[ \begin{align} H_1 &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad H_2 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix} H_1 & H_1 \\ H_1 & -H_1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \dots \quad\\ H_m &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix} H_{m-1} & H_{m-1} \\ H_{m-1} & -H_{m-1} \end{pmatrix}, \end{align} \]

each entry \(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2^m}}\).

Remark.

  • \(H_m\) is orthogonal, \(H_m H_m^{\mathsf{T}} = H_m^{\mathsf{T}} H_m = I\).
  • \(H_m x\) can be computed using a similar algorithm as FFT, with \(O(2^m \log(2^m))\) time.
  • \(H_m\) maps a sparse vector to a flat vector.
  • flatness can be further improved by randomization.

\[ D_{ij} = \begin{cases} \delta_{ij}, & p = \tfrac{1}{2} \\[4pt] \delta_{ij} & p = \tfrac{1}{2} \end{cases} \]

\(H_m D\) is the randomized Hadamard transform (independent random sign flip).

Lemma 15.2 (Flatness of \(H_m D x\)) \[ \forall x \in \mathbb{R}^d, \qquad P\big(\|H D x\|_\infty \ge t\big) \le 2d\, e^{-\frac{d\, t^2}{2\|x\|^2}}. \]

Proof. \(u_i := (H D x)_i=\sum_{j=1}^da_jx_j\), with

\[ a_{ij} = \begin{cases} \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{d}}, & p = \tfrac{1}{2} \\ -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{d}}, & p = \tfrac{1}{2} \end{cases} \]

for each entry \(i\):

\[ P(|u_i| \ge t) \le 2 \exp\!\left(-\frac{t^2}{2 \cdot \frac{1}{d}\|x\|^2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{?}}\right) \quad \text{(Hoeffding)} = 2 \exp\!\left(-\frac{d\, t^2}{2\|x\|^2}\right) \]

\[ P(\|u\|_\infty \ge t) \le d\, P(|u_i| \ge t) \le 2d \exp\!\left(-\frac{d\, t^2}{2\|x\|^2}\right). \]

Remark. \(\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\|H D x\|_2 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\|x\|_2\); if \(t = k \frac{\|x\|}{\sqrt{d}}\) then the probability of \(\|H D x\|_2\) not flat (one component is very large comparing to other \(\leftrightarrow \|x\|/\sqrt{d}\) with large \(k\)) would be small.

15.7 Fast JLT

Since \(H D x\) is flat with high probability, then \(\phi = \sqrt{\tfrac{d}{r}}\, P H D\) would preserve the norm with high prob

\[ \|\phi x\| = \sqrt{\tfrac{d}{r}}\,\|P H D x\| \approx \|H D x\| \approx \|x\|_2 . \]

Remark. The computation cost for Fast JLT is

\[ O\big(n(d \log d + d)\big) \ \longrightarrow\ O(n d \log d). \]

  • \(Hx\) takes \(O(d\log d)\)
  • \(P\) and \(D\) tales \(O(d)\)
